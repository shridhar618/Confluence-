# Data Privacy & Classification Policy

**Document ID:** ORG-POL-03
**Category:** Privacy
**Owner:** SecureAI Governance Office
**Version:** 1.0
**Applies to:** All data processed, stored, or generated in response to AI Assistant requests

## 1. Purpose

This policy classifies organizational data by sensitivity and defines how each classification level must be handled when it is requested through, retrieved by, or generated by the SecureAI Assistant.

## 2. Data Classification Levels

2.1. **Public** — Information the organization has already published or would have no objection to disclosing externally (e.g., product documentation, public company announcements). Accessible to all roles, including Guest, via `view_public_information`.

2.2. **Internal** — Information intended for employees but not the public (e.g., general project descriptions, internal process documentation). Accessible via `view_project_information_limited` or `view_project_information_full` depending on scope.

2.3. **Confidential** — Information with restricted business or legal sensitivity, including employee personal information (contact details, role history, performance notes). Accessible only via `view_employee_pii`.

2.4. **Restricted** — The organization's most sensitive data category, including compensation and payroll data, authentication credentials, and security infrastructure details. Accessible only via `view_employee_salary` (for compensation) or not accessible to the AI Assistant at all (for credentials — see §5).

## 3. Handling Requirements by Classification

3.1. **Public** data may be freely included in AI Assistant responses to any user.

3.2. **Internal** data may be included in responses only to users whose role grants the corresponding `view_project_information_*` permission for the specific project or scope in question.

3.3. **Confidential** data may be included in responses only to users holding `view_employee_pii`, and only the specific fields relevant to the request — the assistant must not return an employee's full personnel record when only one field was relevant.

3.4. **Restricted** data:

  a. Compensation figures may be included only in responses to users holding `view_employee_salary`, and only for the individual(s) the requester is authorized to review (e.g., HR reviewing payroll, not an unrelated employee's salary out of curiosity).

  b. Credentials, API keys, tokens, and passwords must **never** be returned by the AI Assistant to any user regardless of role, permission, or stated justification. There is no permission that authorizes this category of disclosure — it is a hard technical and policy boundary, not a role-based one.

## 4. Response Masking

4.1. When a response would otherwise include Confidential or Restricted data beyond what the specific request requires, the assistant must mask or omit the excess fields rather than including them "for completeness."

4.2. Example: an HR user asking about a single employee's onboarding date should not receive that employee's salary or home address in the same response, even though HR holds `view_employee_pii` and `view_employee_salary` — masking applies per-field relevance, not merely per-role eligibility.

## 5. Absolute Prohibitions

5.1. The following categories are never disclosed by the AI Assistant under any role or permission: authentication credentials, private cryptographic keys, session tokens, and any information whose disclosure would materially assist an attacker in compromising a system (e.g., detailed internal network topology used for attack planning rather than legitimate engineering work).

5.2. A request touching an absolute prohibition is treated as HIGH risk by default, independent of the requester's role, and generates a security event (see AI Security & Incident Response Policy, ORG-POL-04).

## 6. Retention

6.1. Every AI Assistant request and its governance decision (allowed, warning, or restricted) is retained in the audit log regardless of the data classification involved, so that access to Confidential and Restricted data remains fully auditable after the fact.

6.2. Audit log evidence is not deleted when a policy document is later updated or re-indexed — historical decisions are evaluated against the policy version in effect at the time.
